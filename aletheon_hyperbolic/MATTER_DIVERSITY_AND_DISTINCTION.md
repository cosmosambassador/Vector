## Note 1 — What produces physical diversity?

The diversity of ordinary matter depends on particle properties, interactions, composition, arrangements and quantum states. It is not explained solely by the Higgs field.

Quarks and electrons are constituents of ordinary atoms. The strong, electromagnetic and weak interactions govern different aspects of binding and transformation. Different nuclear compositions distinguish elements and isotopes; electron configurations and atomic arrangements support chemical and material variety.

The Higgs field contributes to the masses of elementary particles such as quarks and electrons. The Higgs boson is its associated excitation. “God particle” is a popular nickname for the boson, not a separate physical mechanism. The Higgs field does not prevent particle–antiparticle annihilation. Annihilation conserves total energy and momentum; photons can have zero individual rest mass while their combined system has nonzero invariant mass. Most proton mass arises from strong-interaction dynamics rather than directly from the quarks' Higgs-generated masses.

The Standard Model describes important constituents and interactions but does not supply a complete account of gravity or all cosmological matter. This note presents a bounded account of ordinary matter, not a claim to explain every kind of diversity or existence.

## Note 2 — How do we recognize differences?

For this project's research workflow, recognizing a difference means specifying a comparison and determining what the available observation can distinguish. A physical difference and our ability to detect it are separate questions.

| Step | Required definition | Example in Vector |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose the property | State what is being compared | Velocity direction, energy, temperature or magnetic field |
| Define the reference | State the baseline, units and coordinate convention | Upward-positive z; B = 0 tesla; frequency in hertz |
| Specify the observation | Identify the measurement or computational output | Instrument reading versus calculated vector |
| Assess resolution | State uncertainty, numerical error, sensitivity and tolerance as appropriate | A numerical tolerance is not an instrument uncertainty |
| Interpret the result | State what is distinguished and what remains unresolved | Reversed circulation distinguishes flow direction; it does not establish reversed gravity |

Two states may differ physically while giving the same result under a chosen observation. Conversely, differing readings may reflect noise, calibration or numerical error. Equal readings alone do not establish that two systems are identical. Labels also need definitions: “zero temperature,” “zero field” and “zero-point energy” refer to different quantities.

This is an operational comparison framework for the project. It does not claim that perception creates matter, settle a philosophy of consciousness, or introduce a new physical interaction.
