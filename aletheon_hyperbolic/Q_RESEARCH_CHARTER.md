## Scientific grounding

NHGRI describes synthetic biology as engineering organisms to give them useful new
capabilities. NIST describes engineering biology in terms of intentionally designed
cells, systems and processes, and emphasizes measurement assurance and standards.
Those sources describe actual biological engineering. Vector currently contains
classical software experiments, not engineered biological systems.

Primary institutional references, consulted October 23, 2026:

- NHGRI, Synthetic Biology: https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Synthetic-Biology
- NIST, Engineering/synthetic biology: https://www.nist.gov/engineeringsynthetic-biology
- NIST, Synthetic Biology measurement program: https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/synthetic-biology
- NIST, Synthetic Biology Standards Consortium: https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/synthetic-biology-standards-consortium-sbsc

## Research workflow

1. State one bounded research question and the observable outcome needed to answer it.
2. Enter each scientific claim in an evidence ledger with source, date, method,
   biological system (if any), observation, limitations and evidence classification.
3. Separate computational predictions from biological observations. Record missing
   units, controls, licensing, provenance and uncertainty before analyzing a dataset.
4. For a computational experiment, specify inputs, equations, assumptions, baseline,
   training/development/final-test split, seeds, metrics and reproducibility commands.
5. Report negative results and failed replications. A simulation cannot validate an
   organism or physical interface by itself.
6. Return a concise review to TJ with supported findings and unresolved questions.

Evidence labels: founder-supplied canon; verified repository observation; measured
software result; published biological observation; mathematical derivation;
hypothesis; simulation; unresolved. A primary paper's claim should be attributed
and assessed, rather than treated as independently replicated by Q.
